# Products – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

The Products feature is intended to provide a centralized way to define, organize, maintain, and manage the goods and services handled by an organization.

It serves as a foundation for inventory and other business operations by maintaining consistent product information that can be used across purchasing, sales, inventory management, accounting, and related workflows.

The feature should support different types of products, maintain their identifying and descriptive information, define how they are sold or purchased, and configure the information required to manage them throughout their business lifecycle.

The objective is to ensure that every product has a clear and reliable record that can be referenced by the relevant departments and business processes.

## 2. Purpose and Business Objectives

The Products feature is intended to achieve the following objectives:

- **Centralized Product Management:** Maintain product information in one organized location instead of maintaining separate and inconsistent records across departments.
- **Consistent Product Identification:** Give each product a clear name and unique internal reference, with additional identifiers such as barcodes where applicable.
- **Product Classification:** Organize products into appropriate categories and types to support searching, reporting, and business operations.
- **Sales and Purchasing Support:** Define the information needed to sell products, purchase products, manage suppliers, and apply relevant prices, taxes, and commercial conditions.
- **Inventory Management Support:** Define how physical goods are measured, tracked, stored, and managed within inventory operations.
- **Financial Integration:** Provide product-related accounting information to support appropriate financial recording and valuation.
- **Operational Efficiency:** Reduce repeated data entry, improve information consistency, and make product information accessible to authorized users.
- **Better Decision-Making:** Provide reliable product information that supports inventory planning, purchasing decisions, and operational analysis.

## 3. Main Functional Areas

### 3.1 Product Identification and Basic Information

The feature should allow users to establish the identity of each product through information such as:

- Product name and description.
- Internal reference or product code.
- Barcode or other identification codes.
- Product category.
- Product type, such as goods, services, or combined products where supported.
- Company association or availability across companies.
- Responsible person or user.

Product identification should be clear and consistent so that users can distinguish products and select the correct records during business operations.

### 3.2 Product Classification and Organization

Products should be organized using appropriate categories, tags, and other classification information.

A well-organized product structure should help users:

- Find products quickly.
- Group similar products.
- Distinguish products by business purpose or type.
- Maintain consistent classification across departments.
- Filter and analyze product information efficiently.

The classification structure should be flexible enough to support organizations that manage different types of goods and services.

### 3.3 Product Pricing and Tax Configuration

The feature should support the definition of commercial and financial information associated with products.

This includes:

- Sales prices.
- Product costs.
- Applicable sales taxes.
- Applicable purchase taxes.
- Relevant invoicing policies.
- Other pricing information required by the organization's business processes.

The system should maintain clear relationships between product prices, units of measure, and applicable currencies and taxes.

### 3.4 Sales Information and Configuration

For products involved in sales activities, the feature should provide the information needed to support the sales process.

This may include:

- Whether a product can be sold.
- Sales descriptions and customer-facing information.
- Sales-related warnings or instructions.
- Optional or related products.
- Warranty information.
- Relevant sales policies.

The purpose is to ensure that sales users have the information necessary to select, quote, and sell the correct products under the appropriate conditions.

### 3.5 Purchase and Supplier Information

For products that an organization purchases, the feature should maintain purchasing-related information.

This includes:

- Whether a product can be purchased.
- Supplier or vendor information.
- Supplier-specific product prices.
- Purchase descriptions and instructions.
- Purchase units of measure.
- Applicable purchase taxes.
- Relevant purchasing conditions.

Supplier information should help purchasing users identify appropriate suppliers and make informed procurement decisions.

### 3.6 Inventory and Logistics Configuration

For physical products, the feature should define the information needed to manage them in inventory operations.

This may include:

- Inventory tracking configuration.
- Tracking methods where applicable.
- Units of measure.
- Purchase units of measure.
- Product routes.
- Weight and volume.
- Packaging information.
- Responsible users.
- Shelf-life information where applicable.
- Logistics and handling instructions.
- Descriptions for inventory documents and operations.

These settings should help the organization manage products consistently during receiving, storage, internal movement, and delivery.

### 3.7 Product Attributes and Configurable Characteristics

The feature should support the definition of product characteristics that distinguish products or product options.

Examples of characteristics may include size, material, capacity, color, or other relevant properties.

Where product options are supported, users should be able to define appropriate attributes and values so that products can be described and differentiated consistently.

### 3.8 Product Notes and Documentation

The feature should provide space for relevant product-related information, including:

- Internal notes for employees.
- Sales descriptions.
- Purchase descriptions.
- Customer-facing notes.
- Operational instructions.
- Warranty-related information.

Information should be presented according to its intended audience so that internal instructions are not confused with customer-facing descriptions.

### 3.9 Financial and Accounting Configuration

The feature should support the association of relevant financial accounts with products.

Depending on the organization's accounting configuration, this may include:

- Income accounts.
- Expense accounts.
- Price difference accounts.
- Other product-related accounting settings.

These configurations should support consistent financial recording and coordination between inventory and accounting processes.

### 3.10 Inventory Planning and Replenishment Support

The feature should support product-level planning information that helps organizations maintain appropriate inventory levels.

This may include:

- Reordering information.
- Demand-related information.
- Procurement and replenishment settings.
- Economic Order Quantity (EOQ) information where applicable.
- Other parameters used to support inventory planning.

The purpose is to help the organization reduce unnecessary stock shortages and excess inventory while making purchasing decisions more systematic.

### 3.11 Product Activity and Operational History

Product records should provide access to relevant operational information associated with each product.

This may include records of stock issues and other product-related activities, depending on the organization's configured workflows.

Such information should help authorized users understand how products are being used and identify relevant historical transactions.

## 4. Integration with Other Business Functions

The Products feature is a foundational component that should work consistently with other business functions.

| Business Function | Relationship with Products |
|---|---|
| Inventory | Uses product information for receiving, storage, stock movements, adjustments, and delivery operations. |
| Purchase | Uses product information for supplier selection, purchase orders, and procurement activities. |
| Sales | Uses product information for quotations, orders, pricing, and customer transactions. |
| Accounting | Uses product-related financial configurations to support accounting entries and valuation. |
| Warehouse Operations | Uses product identification, units of measure, logistics, and tracking information during warehouse activities. |
| Inventory Planning | Uses product demand, cost, and replenishment information to support stock planning. |

## 5. Expected Business Benefits

A properly designed and maintained Products feature should provide the following benefits:

- A single, consistent source of product information.
- Improved product identification and classification.
- Reduced duplication and inconsistency in product records.
- More accurate purchasing and sales activities.
- Better coordination between inventory, purchasing, sales, and accounting.
- Improved visibility into product-related information.
- More reliable inventory planning and replenishment.
- Easier product searching, filtering, and reporting.
- Better operational efficiency and data quality.

## 6. Design and Usability Considerations

The Products feature should be designed with simplicity, consistency, and ease of use in mind.

Important considerations include:

- **Clear Organization:** Group related fields into understandable sections and tabs.
- **Relevant Information:** Display fields according to the product type and the user's business needs.
- **Simple Data Entry:** Make product creation straightforward and avoid unnecessary or repetitive information.
- **Appropriate Validation:** Prevent incomplete, inconsistent, or invalid product records.
- **Flexible Configuration:** Support different organizational requirements without making the form unnecessarily complicated.
- **Consistent Terminology:** Use clear field names and descriptions that users can understand.
- **Controlled Access:** Allow authorized users to create, edit, archive, and manage product information according to their responsibilities.
- **Reliable Integration:** Ensure that product information is correctly available to the business processes that depend on it.

## 7. Conclusion

The Products feature should serve as the central foundation for managing the organization's goods and services. It should provide reliable product identification, classification, commercial information, inventory configuration, and financial settings while supporting the business processes that depend on accurate product data.

Its overall goal is to make product information easy to create, maintain, find, understand, and use throughout the organization.
