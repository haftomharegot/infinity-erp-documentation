# Delivery Orders – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

Delivery Orders is an inventory workflow used to manage the outgoing movement of products from an organization's inventory to customers, other destinations, or relevant receiving parties.

It provides a structured process for preparing deliveries, identifying the products and quantities to be delivered, managing delivery readiness, recording completed operations, and maintaining delivery-related information.

Delivery Orders can also be connected to other business processes, allowing delivery activities to originate from related transactions or operational documents.

## 2. Purpose and Business Value

The main purpose of Delivery Orders is to provide control and visibility over outgoing inventory.

The workflow helps organizations to:

- Organize and manage outgoing product deliveries.
- Identify delivery destinations and responsible parties.
- Specify products and quantities required for delivery.
- Monitor delivery schedules and readiness.
- Record delivery completion and related confirmations.
- Maintain information about the origin and progress of delivery operations.
- Review the inventory movements associated with deliveries.

## 3. Main Areas of Delivery Orders

### 3.1 Delivery Order Management

This area covers the creation, organization, and management of delivery orders.

A delivery order generally contains information about the delivery destination, operation type, source location, scheduled date, related source document, and products to be delivered.

### 3.2 Product and Quantity Management

Delivery orders identify the products involved in an outgoing operation and the quantities required.

Depending on the inventory configuration, additional information such as packaging, units of measure, and detailed product movement information may also be relevant.

### 3.3 Delivery Scheduling and Readiness

Scheduling and readiness management help organizations monitor planned deliveries and identify operations that require attention.

This includes monitoring delivery dates, availability, deadlines, and operational status.

### 3.4 Delivery Confirmation and Documentation

Delivery confirmation provides a way to record evidence that a delivery has been acknowledged or signed for.

Delivery-related documentation also supports the preparation and printing of delivery records.

### 3.5 Detailed Inventory Movements

A delivery order is associated with the movement of inventory from a source location.

Detailed movement information provides additional visibility into the products, quantities, locations, and packages involved in the operation.

### 3.6 Returns

Return handling supports the recording and management of products associated with a return from a delivery operation.

The exact return process depends on the organization's inventory procedures and system configuration.

## 4. Relationship with Other Business Functions

Delivery Orders can interact with several business functions:

- **Billing:** Delivery operations may be associated with work orders or other originating business documents.
- **Inventory Management:** Delivery operations update and track outgoing inventory movements.
- **Customer Management:** Delivery information can be associated with customers or other receiving parties.
- **Warehouse Operations:** Source locations, product availability, packaging, and detailed movements support warehouse execution.
- **Document Management:** Delivery records and signatures provide supporting information for operational documentation.

## 5. Conclusion

Delivery Orders provides an organized process for managing outgoing inventory, from preparing delivery requirements to recording operational completion.

It connects delivery information, inventory movements, scheduling, and confirmation activities to support accurate inventory control and better visibility over outgoing products.
