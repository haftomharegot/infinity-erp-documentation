# Manufacturing – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

Manufacturing is an inventory and production management workflow used to plan, organize, and record the transformation of raw materials or components into finished products.

It allows an organization to define what it intends to produce, specify the required components, record production activities, and track the movement of materials and finished goods.

## 2. Purpose and Business Value

The Manufacturing workflow helps organizations:

- Plan and manage production activities.
- Identify the components and quantities required for production.
- Monitor manufacturing progress and completion.
- Track the consumption of components and the production of finished goods.
- Record and manage by-products generated during production.
- Maintain traceability between materials, production orders, and finished products.
- Understand production quantities and related costs.

## 3. Main Areas of Manufacturing

### 3.1 Manufacturing Orders

A manufacturing order represents a planned or authorized production activity. It identifies the product to be manufactured, the required quantity, the scheduled production date, and other relevant production information.

### 3.2 Bill of Materials

A Bill of Materials (BoM) defines the components and quantities required to manufacture a product. It provides the material structure used to plan and manage production.

### 3.3 Components Management

Components are the materials or products consumed during manufacturing. Their quantities and availability are important for determining whether production can proceed.

### 3.4 Work Orders

Work orders represent individual production operations or tasks that may be required to complete a manufacturing order. They help organize and monitor production activities.

### 3.5 By-Products

By-products are additional outputs generated during a manufacturing process alongside the main finished product. Recording them helps maintain a more complete production and inventory record.

### 3.6 Production Tracking and Status Management

Manufacturing orders progress through defined stages as production moves from preparation to completion. Status tracking helps users understand the current state of each order.

### 3.7 Inventory Movements and Traceability

Manufacturing involves inventory movements, including the movement of components into production and finished products out of production. Traceability helps users follow the relationship between production activities, materials, and resulting products.

### 3.8 Manufacturing Cost and Overview

Manufacturing information can be used to review production quantities and related costs, including component costs and overall production costs.

## 4. Relationship with Other Business Functions

Manufacturing is connected to several other business functions:

- **Inventory Management:** Manages component consumption, finished product quantities, and related stock movements.
- **Procurement:** Supports the availability of materials required for production.
- **Product Management:** Provides the product definitions and units used in manufacturing.
- **Cost and Accounting Management:** Supports the review and recording of production-related costs.
- **Warehouse Management:** Supports the movement of components and finished products between relevant locations.

## 5. Conclusion

Manufacturing provides a structured way to manage production activities, from identifying the product and required components to recording production completion. Its connection with inventory, procurement, product management, and cost management helps organizations maintain coordinated production and inventory records.
