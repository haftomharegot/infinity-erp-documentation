# Inventory Reporting – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

Inventory Reporting is the process of collecting, organizing, analyzing, and presenting information about an organization's inventory. It provides visibility into the quantities, locations, movements, availability, and financial value of products held by an organization.

Inventory reports transform inventory transaction data into useful information that supports warehouse operations, purchasing decisions, stock control, financial management, and organizational planning.

An effective inventory reporting system should provide both operational information for day-to-day activities and analytical information that helps managers evaluate inventory performance and make informed decisions.

## 2. Purpose and Business Value

The main purpose of Inventory Reporting is to provide accurate and meaningful information about inventory throughout its lifecycle.

It helps organizations:

- **Monitor stock levels:** Understand the quantities of products available, reserved, incoming, and outgoing.
- **Improve inventory visibility:** Identify where products are stored and how stock is distributed across warehouses and locations.
- **Track inventory movements:** Review receipts, deliveries, transfers, adjustments, and other inventory transactions.
- **Analyze inventory performance:** Identify movement patterns, stock availability trends, and changes in inventory activity.
- **Support financial management:** Understand the monetary value of inventory and support inventory-related financial reporting.
- **Prevent stock shortages and excess inventory:** Identify products that require replenishment or better stock management.
- **Monitor inventory age and expiration:** Identify old, slow-moving, or expiring products that may require attention.
- **Support management decisions:** Provide reliable information for purchasing, storage, distribution, and inventory-control decisions.

By providing these capabilities, Inventory Reporting improves inventory control, operational efficiency, accountability, and decision-making.

## 3. Common Reporting Perspectives

Inventory Reporting provides different perspectives on inventory information to help organizations monitor stock, understand inventory activity, and make informed decisions. The specific reports and analytical capabilities may vary depending on the organization's operations and reporting requirements.

### 3.1. Stock Position and Availability

Provides information about current inventory quantities, reserved stock, available quantities, incoming products, and outgoing requirements. This helps organizations monitor stock levels and identify potential shortages or excess inventory.

### 3.2. Storage and Inventory Traceability

Provides visibility into where products are stored and how inventory is distributed across warehouses and storage locations. Where applicable, reporting may also include package, lot, serial number, and ownership information.

### 3.3. Inventory Movements and Activity

Provides information about inventory receipts, deliveries, internal transfers, adjustments, and other stock movements. Movement analysis can also help organizations identify operational patterns and changes in inventory activity over time.

### 3.4. Inventory Valuation

Provides financial information about the monetary value of inventory, associated product costs, and changes in inventory value. This supports inventory accounting, financial reporting, and reconciliation.

### 3.5. Inventory Aging and Expiration

Provides information about how long products have remained in stock and identifies batches or products approaching or exceeding their expiration dates, where applicable. This helps organizations manage slow-moving, old, and expiring inventory.

### 3.6. Inventory Planning and Forecasting

Provides information about expected future inventory availability, potential stock shortages, and replenishment requirements. Forecasting helps organizations plan purchasing and maintain appropriate stock levels.

## 4. Relationship with Other Business Functions

Inventory Reporting is connected to several organizational functions because inventory information is generated and used throughout business operations.

- **Purchasing:** Provides information about stock availability and replenishment requirements to support purchasing decisions.
- **Warehouse Management:** Provides visibility into stock quantities, locations, movements, and storage conditions.
- **Sales and Distribution:** Helps determine product availability and supports delivery planning.
- **Manufacturing:** Provides information about materials and products consumed or moved during production.
- **Finance and Accounting:** Supports inventory valuation, financial reporting, and reconciliation.
- **Management:** Provides analytical information for evaluating inventory performance and planning organizational resources.

These relationships help ensure that inventory information is consistent and useful across the organization.
