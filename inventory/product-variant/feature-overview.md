# Product Variants – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

Product Variants are different versions or configurations of the same product. They allow an organization to manage products that share a common identity but differ in specific characteristics.

For example, a T-shirt may have different sizes and colors. Instead of registering every combination as an entirely unrelated product, the organization can manage them as variants of one product.

## 2. Product Templates and Variants

A **Product Template** represents the common product definition, while a **Product Variant** represents a specific combination of its available characteristics.

For example:

| Product Template | Variant |
|---|---|
| T-shirt | T-shirt – Small, Red |
| T-shirt | T-shirt – Medium, Blue |
| T-shirt | T-shirt – Large, Black |

Each variant represents a particular product configuration.

## 3. Attributes and Attribute Values

Variants are commonly distinguished using attributes and their values.

- **Attributes:** Characteristics used to differentiate products, such as size, color, material, or capacity.
- **Attribute Values:** The specific options available for an attribute, such as Small, Medium, and Large for size.

Combining different attribute values can produce different product variants.

## 4. Purpose and Benefits

Product Variants help organizations:

- Manage different configurations of the same product.
- Distinguish product options clearly.
- Organize related products under a common product definition.
- Manage variant-specific information where required.
- Avoid treating every product variation as an unrelated product.

## 5. Relationship with Other Business Functions

Product Variants can be used across several business functions:

- **Sales:** Identify and sell the correct product configuration.
- **Purchasing:** Manage purchasing information for relevant variants.
- **Inventory:** Identify and manage inventory quantities for individual variants where applicable.
- **Accounting:** Apply relevant financial configurations to product transactions.

The purpose of Product Variants is to provide a structured way to manage different configurations of the same product while maintaining their relationship with the common product definition.
