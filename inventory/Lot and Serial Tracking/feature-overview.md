 # Lots and Serial Numbers – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

Lots and Serial Numbers are inventory identification and tracking concepts used to distinguish products and individual items throughout their lifecycle.

They help organizations maintain accurate inventory records, identify specific groups of products or individual items, and trace their movement from receipt or production to storage, delivery, use, or disposal.

The two tracking methods serve different purposes:

- **Lot Tracking:** Identifies a group or batch of products using one shared identification number.
- **Serial Number Tracking:** Identifies each individual product item using a unique identification number.

These methods are especially useful for organizations that need detailed inventory traceability, quality control, accountability, and product history.

## 2. Purpose

The main purpose of Lots and Serial Numbers is to improve product identification and inventory traceability.

They help organizations to:

- Identify specific batches or individual products.
- Track products throughout their inventory lifecycle.
- Maintain accurate records of product quantities and movements.
- Identify the source of products when necessary.
- Support quality control and product inspection.
- Manage products with expiration dates.
- Improve accountability and inventory management.
- Support investigations involving damaged, defective, missing, or recalled products.

## 3. Key Concepts

### 3.1. Lot Tracking

Lot tracking is a method of identifying a group or batch of products using a shared identification number.

Products belonging to the same lot generally share relevant characteristics, such as production date, supplier, manufacturing source, or expiration date.

**Example:**

An organization receives 500 units of a particular chemical product. The entire batch is assigned the lot number `CHEM-2026-001`.

If a quality issue is later identified with that batch, the organization can use the lot number to identify the affected products and investigate their inventory history.

**Common applications:**

- Chemicals and raw materials.
- Food and beverages.
- Pharmaceutical products.
- Agricultural products.
- Manufacturing materials.
- Products with expiration dates.

### 3.2. Serial Number Tracking

Serial number tracking is a method of assigning a unique identification number to each individual product item.

Unlike lot tracking, where multiple products share one identification number, every individually tracked item has its own serial number.

**Example:**

An organization receives three generators. Each generator is assigned a unique serial number:

- `GEN-001`
- `GEN-002`
- `GEN-003`

Even though all three generators belong to the same product category, each can be identified and tracked independently.

**Common applications:**

- Computers and electronic equipment.
- Generators and machinery.
- Vehicles and equipment.
- Medical devices.
- High-value products.
- Assets requiring individual maintenance records.

### 3.3. Difference Between Lot and Serial Number Tracking

| Aspect | Lot Tracking | Serial Number Tracking |
|---|---|---|
| Identification | One number identifies a group or batch. | One number identifies an individual item. |
| Quantity | A lot can contain multiple units. | Each serial number represents one individually identified item. |
| Main purpose | Track groups of products with shared characteristics. | Track individual products throughout their lifecycle. |
| Common use | Chemicals, food, raw materials. | Equipment, electronics, machinery. |
| Traceability | Tracks the history of a batch. | Tracks the history of an individual item. |

## 4. Importance of Lot and Serial Number Tracking

### 4.1. Product Traceability

Organizations can identify where products originated, where they have been stored, and how they have moved through the supply chain.

### 4.2. Quality Control

When a quality issue is discovered, organizations can identify the affected batch or individual product and take appropriate action.

### 4.3. Expiration Management

Lot tracking can help organizations monitor expiration dates and identify batches that are approaching or have passed their expiration dates.

### 4.4. Inventory Accuracy

Unique identification helps reduce confusion between similar products and supports more accurate inventory records.

### 4.5. Accountability

Serial numbers make it easier to identify individual equipment and determine its history, location, and responsible department or user.

### 4.6. Product Recall Management

When a product must be recalled, lot and serial number information helps organizations identify the affected products rather than treating all products as one undifferentiated group.

## 6. Expected Benefits

Implementing lot and serial number tracking provides several benefits:

- Improved inventory visibility and traceability.
- Better product identification and accountability.
- More effective quality control.
- Improved management of expired and defective products.
- Easier product recall and investigation.
- More reliable inventory records.
- Better support for maintenance and service activities.
- Reduced risk of mixing products from different batches.
- Improved operational control and decision-making.

## 7. Summary

Lots and Serial Numbers provide organizations with structured methods for identifying and tracking inventory.

Lot tracking focuses on groups or batches of products, while serial number tracking focuses on individual items. Both methods improve inventory traceability, product accountability, quality management, and operational control.

Organizations should select the appropriate tracking method based on the nature of their products and their inventory management requirements.
