# Scrap – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

Scrap is an inventory management process used to record products that are damaged, defective, expired, obsolete, or otherwise unsuitable for normal use.

It removes the scrapped quantity from its source inventory location and records it in a designated scrap location. This helps organizations maintain accurate stock records and distinguish usable inventory from items that have been scrapped.

## 2. Purpose and Business Value

The Scrap feature helps organizations to:

- Record products removed from usable inventory.
- Maintain accurate inventory quantities.
- Identify and document reasons for inventory loss.
- Track the movement of scrapped products.
- Maintain a history of completed scrap operations.
- Support inventory control and accountability.

## 3. Main Information and Operations

A scrap operation generally contains the following information:

- **Product:** The item being scrapped.
- **Quantity:** The amount being removed from usable inventory.
- **Unit of Measure:** The unit used to express the quantity.
- **Scrap Reason:** The reason for scrapping the product.
- **Source Location:** The location from which the product is removed.
- **Scrap Location:** The designated location for scrapped inventory.
- **Package and Owner:** Additional inventory identification information, where applicable.
- **Source Document:** A reference to a related document or operation.

The process typically involves creating a scrap order, providing the required information, and validating the operation.

## 4. Inventory Traceability

Scrap records help organizations trace inventory that has been removed from normal use. Reviewing the associated product movement provides additional information about the product, quantity, source location, destination location, and completion status.

## 5. Relationship with Other Business Functions

Scrap is connected to other inventory activities because it changes the recorded location and status of inventory.

It may also be relevant to:

- **Inventory Reporting:** Understanding inventory losses and stock movements.
- **Product Tracking:** Identifying the affected lot or serial number when tracking is applicable.
- **Finance and Accounting:** Reviewing the financial impact of scrapped inventory.
