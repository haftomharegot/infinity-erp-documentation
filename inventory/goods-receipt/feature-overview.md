# Receipts – Feature Overview

## 1. Overview

The Receipts workflow manages the incoming movement of products into an organization's inventory. It provides a structured way to record, receive, and monitor products arriving from suppliers, customers, other locations, or other business operations.

A receipt records the products being received, their quantities, the destination location, and relevant reference information. It also provides a record of the movement and its progress through the receiving process.

## 2. Purpose and Business Value

The main purpose of the Receipts workflow is to ensure that incoming products are properly recorded and reflected in inventory.

It helps organizations to:

- Record incoming products and quantities.
- Identify the source of incoming products.
- Specify where received products should be stored.
- Monitor the progress of receiving operations.
- Maintain references to related business documents.
- Track completed and pending receipt operations.
- Maintain a record of inventory movements.

## 3. Main Receipt Information

A receipt generally contains the following information:

### 3.1 Transfer Details

Identifies where the products are coming from, the operation type, and the destination location.

### 3.2 Schedule and Reference

Contains the planned receiving date and references to related business documents.

### 3.3 Transfer Summary

Provides an overview of the receipt's status and priority.

### 3.4 Operations

Contains the product lines being received, including product identification, packaging information where applicable, requested quantities, and units of measure.

### 3.5 Additional Information and Notes

Provides space for supplementary operational information and notes related to the receipt.

## 4. Receipt Operations

The receiving process generally involves:

1. Creating a receipt.
2. Identifying the source and destination of the incoming products.
3. Adding the products and quantities to be received.
4. Reviewing the receipt information.
5. Processing and validating the receipt.
6. Confirming the completed inventory movement.

The exact steps and required validations may vary depending on the organization's receiving procedures and configuration.

## 5. Receipt Monitoring and Related Operations

A receipt workflow may also provide supporting capabilities such as:

- **Search and Filtering:** Locating receipts using relevant information and operational conditions.
- **Status Monitoring:** Identifying receipts at different stages of processing.
- **Detailed Operations:** Reviewing the detailed inventory movement lines associated with a receipt.
- **Returns:** Recording products returned through a return-related inventory operation.
- **Activity History:** Reviewing changes and activities associated with a receipt.
- **Printing:** Producing receipt documentation where required.

## 6. Relationship with Other Business Functions

The Receipts workflow is connected to other business activities that involve incoming inventory.

Examples include:

- **Purchase Management:** Receiving products associated with purchase transactions.
- **Sales and Customer Operations:** Recording products returned by customers.
- **Internal Inventory Operations:** Receiving products transferred from other internal locations.
- **Inventory Management:** Updating inventory records based on completed receiving operations.
- **Accounting and Finance:** Supporting the recording and reconciliation of inventory-related transactions where applicable.

## 7. Conclusion

The Receipts workflow provides an organized way to record and manage incoming inventory. By capturing the source, destination, products, quantities, references, and processing status, it supports accurate inventory records and better control over the receiving process.
